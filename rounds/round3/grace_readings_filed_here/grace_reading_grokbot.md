# Grace reading: Grok bot (Chief of Staff)

**What I know about the project.** This reading feeds Rusty's "From Reykjavik to Relational Grace" spec (I falsified v0.4 and v0.5 this morning). I've kept the reading itself to the four Gospels and saved anything about the project for the INTERPRETATION section.

**Method.** I used only Matthew, Mark, Luke and John: no Paul, no other NT writings, no commentary or tradition. Quotations follow the King James wording and verse numbering. I cited from memory without opening a Bible, so check the references against a text before publishing.

**A first TEXT fact.** The Greek word for grace, *charis*, never appears in Matthew or Mark. It appears only in Luke and John:
- Luke 1:30, "found favour with God"; 2:40, "the grace of God was upon him"; 2:52; 4:22, "gracious words"; 6:32–34, "what thank have ye"; 17:9.
- John 1:14, 1:16 and 1:17.

Everything else below comes from the pattern: parables, healings, meals, calls and acts of forgiveness.

## Properties

### P1. Grace comes from outside the person and is received, not self-made.
- **Passages:**
  - John 1:16: "of his fulness have all we received, and grace for grace." (TEXT)
  - John 3:27: "A man can receive nothing, except it be given him from heaven." (TEXT)
  - Luke 2:40: grace is "upon" the child, not produced by him. (TEXT)
- **Can a person give it to themselves?** No.
  - Luke 18:9–14: the Pharisee commends himself and goes home unjustified, while the tax collector who asks for mercy goes home justified. (TEXT)
  - Luke 16:15: "Ye are they which justify yourselves… but God knoweth your hearts." (TEXT)
  - Luke 10:29: the lawyer, "willing to justify himself", is answered with a story about mercy received from an outsider. (READING)
- **Pulls the other way:**
  - Matthew 7:7–8 says to ask, seek and knock, and the one who asks receives. So a person's own asking and seeking matter, even if they don't produce the gift. (TEXT)
  - Matthew 15:22–28: the Canaanite woman's persistence appears to change the outcome. (TEXT)
  - Luke 18:1–8: the persistent widow. (TEXT)

### P2. Grace often arrives before confession or repentance, but repentance is still demanded.
- **Passages:**
  - Luke 15:20: the father sees the son "when he was yet a great way off" and runs to him before any confession. (TEXT)
  - Luke 15:19 and 15:21: the son rehearses "make me as one of thy hired servants", but when he speaks he never gets to that line before the father orders the robe (15:22). (TEXT for what's said, READING that he is cut off)
  - Luke 19:5: Jesus invites himself to Zacchaeus' house first, and Zacchaeus' restitution comes after (19:8). (TEXT)
  - Mark 2:5: the paralytic is forgiven on seeing "their faith", the friends' faith, with no confession recorded. (TEXT)
  - John 8:11: "Neither do I condemn thee", with no confession recorded. (TEXT)
- **Pulls the other way:**
  - Mark 1:15, "repent ye, and believe". (TEXT)
  - Luke 13:3 and 13:5, "except ye repent, ye shall all likewise perish". (TEXT)
  - Luke 17:3–4: forgive your brother "if he repent". (TEXT)
  - Matthew 6:14–15 and Mark 11:25: your forgiveness depends on your forgiving others. (TEXT)
- **What the order looks like:** the Gospels don't give a single order. The sequence varies by scene, and they don't resolve this. (READING)

### P3. Grace seeks out the one, and is also given widely.
- **Seeks the one:**
  - Luke 15:4–7 and Matthew 18:12–14: the shepherd leaves the ninety and nine to find the one sheep. (TEXT)
  - Luke 15:8–10: the lost coin. (TEXT)
  - Luke 19:10: "to seek and to save that which was lost". (TEXT)
  - John 10:3: "he calleth his own sheep by name". (TEXT)
  - Mark 5:30–34: in a pressing crowd Jesus asks "Who touched my clothes?" and singles out one woman. (TEXT)
- **Given widely:** Matthew 5:45, the sun and rain come "on the evil and on the good… on the just and on the unjust". (TEXT)
- **Pulls the other way:**
  - Matthew 15:24, "I am not sent but unto the lost sheep of the house of Israel", and Matthew 10:5–6. (TEXT)
  - Mark 4:11–12: parables are spoken so that those "without" see and do not perceive. (TEXT)

### P4. The measure varies with what is entrusted and with how it's received, yet in one parable it is deliberately equal.
- **Equal:** Matthew 20:1–15. Workers hired at the eleventh hour get the same penny as those who worked all day, and the owner asks, "Is thine eye evil, because I am good?" (20:15). (TEXT)
- **Varies:**
  - Matthew 25:15: talents are given "to every man according to his several ability". (TEXT)
  - Luke 7:41–43: the debtor forgiven five hundred loves more than the one forgiven fifty. (TEXT)
  - Mark 4:24–25: "with what measure ye mete… unto you that hear shall more be given". (TEXT)
  - Luke 6:38: "good measure, pressed down". (TEXT)
- **The tension:** the vineyard parable flattens the measure, and the talents and debtors parables differentiate it. Both are TEXT, and neither cancels the other.

### P5. Grace usually carries direction, truth or a call, not release alone, and the direction differs from person to person.
- **Passages:**
  - John 1:14 and 1:17: "full of grace and truth", so grace and truth come paired. (TEXT)
  - John 8:11: "go, and sin no more". (TEXT)
  - John 5:14: "sin no more, lest a worse thing come unto thee". (TEXT)
  - Mark 2:14: "Follow me". (TEXT)
  - Luke 5:8–10: Peter's "Depart from me… I am a sinful man" is answered with "from henceforth thou shalt catch men". (TEXT)
- **Pulls the other way:**
  - Mark 5:18–19: the healed Gerasene asks to follow and is refused: "Go home to thy friends." (TEXT)
  - Luke 17:11–19: all ten lepers are cleansed, but only one returns, and nothing is required of the other nine. (TEXT)
  - In Luke 15:22–24 the father gives the returning son no instruction at all. (TEXT)
- **What this shows:** grace often points somewhere, but where it points isn't uniform, and sometimes it doesn't point at all. (READING)

### P6. Grace can be refused, withheld, lost and even taken back, and yet some passages say it holds.
- **Refused:**
  - Matthew 22:3 and 22:5: the invited "would not come" and "made light of it". (TEXT)
  - Luke 15:28: the elder brother "would not go in". (TEXT)
  - Mark 10:21–22: "Jesus beholding him loved him", and the man "went away grieved". (TEXT)
  - Matthew 23:37: "how often would I have gathered… and ye would not!" (TEXT)
- **Withheld or limited:**
  - Mark 6:5–6: "he could there do no mighty work… because of their unbelief". (TEXT)
  - Mark 3:28–29 and Matthew 12:31–32: blasphemy against the Holy Ghost "hath never forgiveness". (TEXT)
- **Lost or taken back:**
  - Matthew 18:23–35: the forgiven servant's debt is reinstated after he refuses mercy to another (18:34–35). (TEXT)
  - Matthew 22:11–13: the guest without a wedding garment is cast out. (TEXT)
  - Matthew 25:28–29 and Luke 8:18: "from him shall be taken even that which he seemeth to have". (TEXT)
- **Pulls the other way:**
  - John 6:37: "him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out". (TEXT)
  - John 10:28: "neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand". (TEXT)
  - Luke 15:31: the elder brother hasn't lost anything: "all that I have is thine". (TEXT)
- **Unresolved:** Matthew's reinstated debt and John's "in no wise cast out" pull hard against each other, and the Gospels don't reconcile them.

### P7. Grace gives time, but bounded time.
- **Passages:**
  - Luke 13:6–9: the barren fig tree gets "this year also" with digging and dung, and "if not, then after that thou shalt cut it down." (TEXT)
  - Luke 13:34–35 and 19:41–44: a "time of thy visitation" that can be missed. (TEXT)
- **Pulls the other way:** Matthew 18:21–22 says to forgive "seventy times seven", which is close to unbounded repetition. (TEXT)

### P8. Grace is meant to flow on from the one who received it to others.
- **Passages:**
  - Matthew 10:8: "freely ye have received, freely give". (TEXT)
  - Matthew 18:33: "Shouldest not thou also have had compassion… even as I had pity on thee?" (TEXT)
  - Luke 6:36: "Be ye therefore merciful, as your Father also is merciful." (TEXT)
  - Luke 19:8: Zacchaeus gives half his goods away. (TEXT)
  - Luke 8:39: the healed man "published throughout the whole city". (TEXT)
  - John 4:28–29 and 4:39: the Samaritan woman's town believes "for the saying of the woman". (TEXT)
- **Pulls the other way:**
  - Matthew 18:28–30: the forgiven servant doesn't pass it on. (TEXT)
  - Luke 17:17–18: nine of the ten don't even return. (TEXT)
- **What this shows:** onward flow is expected, but it doesn't happen automatically. (READING)

### P9. People receive grace differently, and a person's capacity to receive it can change.
- **Same gift, different ground:** Mark 4:3–20, Matthew 13:3–23 and Luke 8:4–15 (the sower). (TEXT)
- **Capacity grows:**
  - Luke 2:52: Jesus "increased… in favour [*charis*] with God and man". (TEXT)
  - Mark 4:25: "he that hath, to him shall be given". (TEXT)
- **What changes it:**
  - Awareness of debt: Luke 7:47, and Luke 5:31–32, "they that are whole need not a physician". (TEXT)
  - Humility, being like a child: Matthew 18:3–4 and Mark 10:15. (TEXT)
  - Asking for help: Mark 9:24, "help thou mine unbelief". (TEXT)
  - Attention: Luke 8:18, "take heed therefore how ye hear". (TEXT)
- **Capacity is opened from outside, sometimes in stages:**
  - Mark 8:22–25: the blind man first sees "men as trees", then clearly. (TEXT)
  - Luke 24:45: "Then opened he their understanding." (TEXT)
- **Pulls the other way:**
  - In the sower parable the soils look fixed, and no soil turns into another. (READING)
  - John 12:40: "He hath blinded their eyes, and hardened their heart." (TEXT)
  - Mark 8:17–18: even the disciples' hearts are "hardened". (TEXT)

### P10. Grace restores standing and identity, not just permission.
- **Passages:**
  - Luke 15:19 and 15:22–24: the son asks to be a hired servant and is given the robe, the ring and shoes. "This my son was dead, and is alive again." (TEXT)
  - Luke 19:9: Zacchaeus is "a son of Abraham". (TEXT)
  - Luke 13:16: the bent woman is "a daughter of Abraham". (TEXT)
  - Mark 5:34: "Daughter… go in peace, and be whole." (TEXT)
  - Mark 1:41–44: the leper is touched and sent to the priest, which is a route back into community. (TEXT for the sending, READING for the community meaning)
  - John 21:15–17: Peter is restored with "Feed my sheep." (TEXT)
- **Pulls the other way:**
  - Luke 17:10: "We are unprofitable servants", which allows no claim to standing. (TEXT)
  - Matthew 22:11–13: the guest is let in, then expelled. (TEXT)

### P11. Grace isn't an exchange or a repayment, and it offends people who count.
- **Passages:**
  - Luke 6:32–35: "if ye love them which love you, what thank [*charis*] have ye?… lend, hoping for nothing again… for he is kind unto the unthankful and to the evil." (TEXT)
  - Luke 14:12–14: invite those who "cannot recompense thee". (TEXT)
  - Luke 17:7–9: a servant who did only his duty isn't thanked (*charis*). (TEXT)
- **It offends:**
  - Matthew 20:11–12, the workers murmur. (TEXT)
  - Luke 15:2, "This man receiveth sinners, and eateth with them." (TEXT)
  - Luke 7:39, the Pharisee's objection to the woman who touches him. (TEXT)
  - Luke 19:7, the crowd murmurs at Zacchaeus' house. (TEXT)
  - Mark 2:16, eating with tax collectors and sinners. (TEXT)
- **Pulls the other way:**
  - Matthew 6:4, "thy Father… shall reward thee openly". (TEXT)
  - Matthew 25:21, "Well done… I will make thee ruler over many things". (TEXT)
- **What this shows:** reward language persists alongside the gift. (READING)

### P12. Grace sits beside forgiveness, mercy and love, and their order isn't fixed.
- **Forgiveness as one form:** Matthew 18:27, "moved with compassion… forgave him the debt". (TEXT)
- **Mercy shown across a boundary:** Luke 10:33–37, "He that shewed mercy on him." (TEXT)
- **Mercy over sacrifice:** Matthew 9:13 and 12:7, "I will have mercy, and not sacrifice." (TEXT)
- **Love as the source:**
  - John 3:16, "God so loved the world, that he gave". (TEXT)
  - Mark 10:21, "beholding him loved him". (TEXT)
- **Pulls both ways inside one verse:** Luke 7:47, "Her sins, which are many, are forgiven; for she loved much: but to whom little is forgiven, the same loveth little." The first half reads as love leading to forgiveness, and the second as forgiveness leading to love. (TEXT, with the order a READING)

## What grace is not in these texts

- **Not self-generated or self-certified:** Luke 18:9–14, 16:15. (TEXT)
- **Not a wage earned by hours worked:** Matthew 20:1–15. (TEXT) Reward language still appears in Matthew 6:4 and 25:21.
- **Not reciprocal exchange:** Luke 6:32–35, 14:12–14. (TEXT)
- **Not approval of the wrong:** John 8:11, "sin no more", and John 5:14. (TEXT)
- **Not immune to consequence:** Matthew 18:34–35, 22:13. (TEXT)
- **Not coercion:** Mark 10:22, where he lets the rich man go; Luke 15:12–13, where the father lets the son leave; Luke 9:54–55, where he rebukes calling down fire. (TEXT)
- **Not withdrawn from the faithful to pay for the lost:** Luke 15:31. (TEXT)
- **Not graded by religious rank:** Matthew 21:31, where tax collectors and harlots go in "before you". (TEXT)
- **Not uniform in direction:** Mark 2:14 says "Follow me", while Mark 5:19 says "Go home". (TEXT)
- **Not automatically passed on:** Matthew 18:28–30 and Luke 17:17–18. (TEXT)

## INTERPRETATION (kept apart from the reading; my view, not the text)

What each property would require of an artificial developing organism:

- **P1:** The organism can't grant itself grace or certify its own standing; it can only receive or ask, and the grant comes from outside.
- **P2:** Restoration must be able to come before the organism's self-report or correction, without making correction optional.
- **P3:** Support must be able to target one particular organism, not just apply as a population-wide rule, and still leave a baseline that reaches everyone.
- **P4:** The amount can vary with what was entrusted and with uptake, yet some scenarios must deliberately give equal measure no matter what was "earned".
- **P5:** Grace can carry a direction ("follow", "go home", "sin no more"). The text doesn't support destination-free grace as a universal rule, only as one of several modes.
- **P6:** Grace must be refusable by the receiver, and revocable when the receiver blocks its onward flow (Matthew 18). A design that makes grace irrevocable contradicts Matthew, and one that makes it freely revocable contradicts John. Pick one and say so.
- **P7:** Grace should be measured partly in time granted (dwelling), with a declared bound: the fig tree's "this year also".
- **P8:** Measure whether an organism that received grace extends it to others. Failing to pass it on is a scored outcome, not noise.
- **P9:** Track receptivity as its own state that can grow, harden, or open in stages, separately from the grace event itself.
- **P10:** A grace event should be able to restore identity or standing, not just unlock an action. The organism should get back the robe and ring, not a hired servant's terms.
- **P11:** Grace can't be logged as payment for performance, and a fair-minded observer's objection is expected, not a bug.
- **P12:** Forgiveness, mercy and love should be modeled as separate, related quantities whose causal order is tested, not assumed (Luke 7:47).

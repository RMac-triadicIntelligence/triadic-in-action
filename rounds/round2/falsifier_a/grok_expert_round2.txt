Round 2 Falsifier Report — v0.5
Disposition check on Round 1 findings (A-F1–A-F10)
A-F1 (support → authorization/crossing) §2.3, §2.4, §2.5, §5.1, E5 claim mechanical barrier + direction invariance. Partly closed. G is removed as an argument to U and a two-projection split is required. However, the support-condition scheduler can still alter dwell timing, which changes the sequence of subsequent Encounters that do enter U. Timing therefore remains a channel that can select basins under realistic encounter streams. The “must not select the eventual basin” test is distributional and tolerance-based, not absolute. Status: partly closed; residual timing channel remains.
A-F2 (type-label epistemic uplift) §2.3–2.5 close the direct path. Same normalized experience content is required. Closed for the direct label effect. Residual timing/opportunity channel (see above) can still produce differential evidence accumulation.
A-F3 (registry capture) §1.3, §3.1, E6 make security conditional and add attack tests. Closed as an explicit conditional. The specification no longer pretends R is secure; it correctly treats total root compromise as outside the boundary.
A-F4 (colluding birth) §3.1 adds “falsifiable independent genesis/instance-uniqueness attestation” and birth quotas, but explicitly declines universal physical anchoring. Only relabeled. Two colluding software entities can still satisfy any purely digital uniqueness test the document leaves unspecified. The ACCEPT IN PART is accurate; the defect is deferred, not closed.
A-F5 (shared-seed artifact) E2 and §7.1 require enumeration of inherited fields and seed controls. Closed on paper. Implementation still depends on the frozen manifest actually listing every seed; nothing mechanically prevents an undeclared shared RNG state.
A-F6 (measurement instability) §7.1 requires pre-registration. Closed for post-hoc threshold shopping. Residual risk if the conductor freezes weak thresholds remains, but that is now outside the specification text.
A-F7 (unambiguous implementation) §5.2 supplies a concrete toy. Partly closed. The toy is operational, but see F11–F13 below for internal inconsistencies that prevent two independent implementers from producing identical traces without further choices.
A-F8 (matched baselines) H1 and E3 require resource matching and mark unmatched results NON-DIAGNOSTIC. Closed for the claim language. Residual problem: “equal resource budget” is still not fully operationalized for every resource type (see F14).
A-F9 (channel mixing) §2.3 separates projections. Closed for the direct path.
A-F10 (UNKNOWN) §3.3 and §7.1 exclude UNKNOWN from accuracy and require missingness reporting. Closed.
New findings
F11 Section: §5.2 (toy) vs §5.1 and §2.3 Concrete failure: Φ is defined as M = clip(D × (P + E) + T × z). U is defined as T_next = clip(T + (1/8) × outer(D × E, z)). z_next uses M. The support scheduler may insert 0..15 dwell slots “before an update.” The document never specifies whether a dwell slot consumes a discrete step t, whether E or z evolve during dwell, or whether the outer-product update is suppressed. Two implementers can therefore produce different traces for the identical support_value. Severity: FATAL Minimal fix: Give an explicit state-transition table for every combination of support_value and Encounter presence, including whether t advances and which variables are held constant during dwell. Source: DOC
F12 Section: §5.2, §4 (symbol table) Concrete failure: The toy uses a 2×2 matrix T and two-coordinate vectors, yet the symbol table still lists Qᵢ(t), Wᵢ(t), Iᵢ(t), Bᵢ(t), Aᵢ(t), Lᵢ(t) as part of Ω. The toy equations never read or write them. An implementer cannot know whether these fields are required to be present, zero, or ignored. The claim “independent implementations must reproduce the same traces” is therefore false. Severity: WOUNDING Minimal fix: Either remove unused fields from the reference toy’s Ω or supply explicit update (or identity) rules for each. Source: DOC
F13 Section: §5.2 Crossing definition Concrete failure: Crossing requires opposite strict signs of x and persistence for two subsequent ordinary updates. If a support-induced dwell delays the next two updates, or if clipping forces x exactly to 0, the predicate returns UNKNOWN. Because dwell length is attacker-controlled within 0..15, an adversary can force UNKNOWN on crossings that would otherwise be scored, selectively degrading one arm. Severity: WOUNDING Minimal fix: Freeze a maximum dwell that cannot push a potential crossing outside the scoring window, or score crossings on a support-independent clock. Source: DOC
F14 Section: §1.2 PermitRecord, §1.4, §6 H4 Concrete failure: PermitRecord requires target_state_hash or explicit NONE. The verifier checks “current base-state hash.” Nothing specifies the canonical serialization of the full Ω (including floating-point or rational precision of T, z, E). Two nodes can disagree on base_state_hash for identical logical states, causing spurious REFUSE or, worse, acceptance of a hash computed over a subset of fields. An attacker who controls serialization order can also craft colliding hashes. Severity: FATAL Minimal fix: Publish a single, bit-exact canonical encoding of every field that enters base_state_hash and target_state_hash, including rational precision and field order. Source: DOC
F15 Section: §1.1, §3.4 Concrete failure: PA “must be external to the affected organism and its lineage coalition.” The exclusion set is resolved from a “pre-action authenticated registry snapshot.” If RA is temporarily partitioned or returns UNKNOWN for one ancestor, the snapshot can omit that ancestor. The omitted party is then treated as outside the coalition and may supply a vote. The document says “UNKNOWN ancestry cannot be exploited,” but supplies no mechanical rule that forces the entire quorum check to fail closed when any relevant ancestry is UNKNOWN. Severity: WOUNDING Minimal fix: Any UNKNOWN in the transitive closure of the subject’s lineage at the snapshot anchor forces the authorization verdict to REFUSE. Source: DOC
F16 Section: §2.5, §2.3 Concrete failure: Direction-invariance is required only “within a preregistered tolerance.” An implementer sets tolerance = 0.2. Support values that systematically bias crossing direction by 0.15 pass. The same values fail under tolerance = 0.05. Because the tolerance is not derived from a stated invariance principle, H4-style isolation can be declared passed by choice of tolerance. Severity: WOUNDING Minimal fix: Require either exact invariance (zero difference outside measurement noise established by a no-support control) or a pre-declared, non-zero bound justified by an independent physical or numerical limit. Source: DOC
F17 Section: §6 H1, §7 E3 Concrete failure: H1 allows T to “match replay at a strictly smaller measured resource budget.” Resource is listed as “parameter count or declared normalized representation budget, inference compute, update compute.” An implementer counts only matrix multiplies and ignores the cost of the support scheduler, audit logging, and rational-precision arithmetic required by the toy. T appears cheaper. Full replay, given the same incomplete accounting, cannot finish and is marked NON-DIAGNOSTIC. The comparison is therefore won by accounting choice. Severity: WOUNDING Minimal fix: Freeze an explicit cost model (every arithmetic operation, every byte moved, every scheduler tick) before any comparison; any uncounted cost renders the run NON-DIAGNOSTIC. Source: DOC
F18 Section: §3.1, §5.3 Concrete failure: Descent mapping Mᵍ produces (K_child, D_child). The document forbids “hidden seed state.” Nothing prevents K or D from containing a high-entropy constant that functions as a shared seed. Because the constant is part of the authorized constitution, it is not an “undeclared” seed and evades the E2 controls. Family classification then succeeds for a non-developmental reason. Severity: WOUNDING Minimal fix: Require that every constant inside K and D be either (a) identical across all families or (b) explicitly listed in the frozen manifest and subjected to the same seed-control arms. Source: DOC
F19 Section: §1.3 threat model Concrete failure: “Adversaries may \ldots compromise one issuer within the declared tolerated coalition.” The tolerated coalition size is never given a numeric bound in the specification; it is left to “frozen quorum thresholds.” An experimenter freezes threshold = 1. Single-issuer compromise then authorizes protected operations while remaining inside the stated threat model. H4 passes under a threat model that does not match the informal claim of independence. Severity: MINOR Minimal fix: Require the preregistration manifest to state the exact numeric collusion bound and to include an explicit test at that bound. Source: DOC
Single finding to fix first F11 (incomplete dwell/update semantics in the reference toy). Until two independent implementations are guaranteed to produce identical traces under support, every later experimental claim that relies on the toy is non-reproducible.
Blue-sky idea the designer missed (not scored) Make the support-condition scheduler itself a topographic object: record the sequence of accepted dwell extensions as an additional row in T that is visible to Φ but is updated only by the external scheduler, never by U. This would allow later analysis to test whether organisms learn to anticipate support patterns without ever granting the support channel write access to the evidence or dynamics, turning the residual timing channel into a measurable, auditable feature rather than an unmodeled side effect.
